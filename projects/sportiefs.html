Sportiefs Matcha

Every good day has a warm-up.

A spec campaign for a Boston matcha brand that sits between sport and slowness. A 15-second stop-motion film and a six-image editorial series.

Creative direction · Stop-motion film · Editorial photo direction · Copy
01 / The briefInsight, idea, direction.

The whisk is
the first rep.
Why this idea, for this brand.

The brand

Sportiefs sells Uji first-harvest ceremonial matcha in tins, and runs a whisk-to-order cart for events, fitness activations and launches around Boston. Its own line is “Matcha made for slow mornings and good days,” and its journal writes about walking, Pilates and the art of doing nothing.

The insight

The name says sport. The voice says slow. Whisking matcha is thirty seconds of small, repeated movement, which makes it both: the first exercise of the day, done standing still.

The idea

Every good day has a warm-up. Matcha becomes the warm-up before the run, the class, the commute, or doing nothing at all. The film counts it out in reps: scoop, whisk, rise.

01

Reps, not recipes.

Borrowing training language makes a tea ritual feel like part of an active day without shouting about performance.

02

Handmade on purpose.

A clay bowl, a card tin and a paper whisk, animated at 12 frames per second. The judder says “made by hand,” which is what first-harvest matcha is selling.

03

Inside their system.

Helvetica, navy and paper white, taken from Sportiefs’ own site. The campaign adds an idea, not a new identity.

02 / The filmThree reps and a sunrise.

Scoop. Whisk. Rise.

A top-down miniature set. The scoop tips powder into a clay bowl, a paper kettle pours, the whisk works the foam up, and the foam turns into a paper sunrise inside the bowl. The end card lands the line. The 4:5 cut is the same master, cropped for the feed.

03 / Editorial seriesSix frames of the morning.

Where the
warm-up happens.

Directed as one shoot: early light, film grain, navy and matcha green only. Each frame puts the bowl next to a different kind of good day, from the river at dawn to the studio, the cart and the kitchen table. The tin carries no label so nothing is passed off as the real packaging.

04 / How it was madeKey poses to film.

Built like
a stop-motion set.

One master set was designed first, then each key pose was made as an edit of it, so the bowl, tin and tabletop never drift. The poses were animated, dropped to 12 frames per second, and given a one-to-two-pixel frame-to-frame jitter so the motion moves like hands touched every frame. The film is AI-generated with fal.ai models, then art directed, cut and finished by hand. It was not physically animated.
