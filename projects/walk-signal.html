Walk Signal

An LA crosswalk figure realises the countdown is almost over, and runs for it.

A short street-detail animation. The little walking figure in an LA pedestrian signal speeds up as the countdown drops, starts sweating at 3, and at 1 jumps right out of the display. The orange hand is left behind to hold the corner.

['Concept', 'Animation', 'Creative coding', 'Compositing']
01 / The briefConcept and decisions.

The idea.
Why this idea, for this brand.

The genre

Inspired by street-detail animations that bring walk-signal figures to life (the genre of hellominamir); original LA scene, figure animation and timing.

The insight

Everyone in LA knows the feeling of a countdown hitting 3 when you're halfway across six lanes. I gave that feeling to the figure on the sign. The joke only works if the signal itself looks completely real, so the street is a photo-real plate and every LED in the animation sits on the display's real dot grid.

The idea

An LA crosswalk figure realises the countdown is almost over, and runs for it.

01

Real plate, coded LEDs

The pole and street are a single generated photograph with the display left dark. The figure, digits and hand are code drawn dot by dot, so the timing is exact and nothing morphs.

02

Animated by the beat

The figure walks calmly from 7 to 4, picks up the pace with a sweat drop at 3, sprints with speed lines at 2, and jumps out on 1. The countdown carries the story without a single word.

03

Handheld, not locked off

A slow, layered drift and light grain make it feel like a phone pointed up at a real corner, not a motion-graphics mockup.

02 / The workFrames and cuts.
03 / The processHow it was made.
STEP 01

Street plate

Generated one 9:16 photo with Nano Banana 2.1: a yellow pedestrian signal on a pole, palm trees, an LA storefront, and the display deliberately unlit. The first take was used.

STEP 02

LED figure as code

Wrote an HTML canvas page that draws a jointed walking figure at low resolution and reads it out as glowing LED dots on the display's grid. The gait speed, lean and arm swing are tied to the countdown, alongside a 5×7 dot-matrix font and the orange hand.

STEP 03

Frame-by-frame render

Rendered 300 transparent frames (10 s at 30 fps) with headless Chrome, in parallel. Two frames that crashed were re-rendered.

STEP 04

Composite and drift

Composited the LED frames over the plate in ffmpeg, punched in 1.35× on the signal, added a sinusoidal handheld drift and light grain, and added the AI-generated mark. No AI video model was used.

STEP 05

Second pass: a real pixel walker

In the first cut the figure was drawn from stick limbs that were too thick for the LED grid, so it read as a blob. I replaced it with hand-made pixel sprites at double scale: two walk frames, two run frames with bent arms and a forward lean, and a jump pose.

What it is not

The street photograph is AI-generated (Google Nano Banana 2.1 via fal) and is not a real location. The LED animation was hand-coded in HTML canvas, rendered with headless Chrome and composited with ffmpeg. AI-generated imagery is labelled in the video. A self-initiated piece inspired by a genre, not a copy of any specific work.
