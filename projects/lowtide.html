LOW TIDE

Come in
at low tide.

A coastal natural-wine bar identity built on a brush-script sign, enamel green and a very short menu.

Brand identity · Menu system · Signage
01 / The ideaStart with a reason.

A bar that keeps
the tide's hours.

The brief

Create an identity for a small neighborhood wine bar by the water — warm, unfussy, recognizable from the boardwalk — with a menu system short enough to read at a glance.

The response

The name sets the pace: things slow down at low tide. A loose, hand-painted brush script gives the bar a sign that looks like it has always been there; enamel green, oyster white and a single coral underline keep the palette coastal without a nautical cliché. The menu is one folded card.

01

Paint the name, don't typeset it.

A brush-script wordmark reads as hospitality rather than a brand. It survives chalk, enamel and matchbook foil equally well.

02

One card, three columns.

Glass, bottle, dozen. The entire menu system is one folded card with three prices, so the bar never needs a laminated list.

03

Let the enamel age.

Sea-glass green is chosen to look better scuffed. The identity is designed for salt air, not for a screen.

02 / The identityMark, color, hierarchy.

Script for the sign.
Grotesk for the prices.

The brush script appears exactly once per surface. Everything else — prices, hours, the oyster count — is set in a small, even grotesk so the sign stays the only loud thing in the room.

Fixed

Brush-script wordmark, sea-glass field, one coral underline, oyster-white type.

Variable

Chalk versus enamel versus foil, the day's three prices, the oyster of the week.

Format rule

Sign: script alone. Menu card: script small, prices large. Matchbook: script and nothing else.
