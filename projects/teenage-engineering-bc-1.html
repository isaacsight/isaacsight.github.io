Teenage Engineering
EP/BC-1

Everyone’s listening.

A vertical loop for teenage engineering’s broadcast CD player. The machine is the brand’s own studio footage, untouched: the disc really spins and the counter really runs. Around it, hand-drawn listeners lounge, sway and grow flowers out of the speaker grille, and the disc wears a custom label that turns once per bar.

01 / The briefInsight, idea, direction.

The product is
the proof.
Why this idea, for this brand.

The brand

teenage engineering makes objects people photograph before they use them. The EP/BC-1 is a professional CD player that snaps onto the side of a speaker: a keypad, red LED counters, a blue PLAY key and a disc that spins in plain view.

The insight

The fastest way to lose a gear audience is to show them a fake machine. Every key, label and red digit on this player is checked by people who own one, so the product had to stay real.

The idea

Everyone’s listening. Leave the machine exactly as teenage engineering filmed it, and let a cast of drawn listeners do the feeling: lying on it, leaning on it, nodding along while the sound blooms out of the grille as flowers.

01

Real machine, drawn world.

Every pixel of the player, disc, LEDs and reflection comes from the brand’s own studio clip. The cartoon layer never covers the control panel.

02

Art on the moving part.

The one thing that moves on a CD player is the disc, so that is where the art goes: a label printed onto the real spinning disc, lit by the studio light and turning once per bar.

03

Timed to the track.

The loop is eight bars at 92 BPM. One slow swing of the giant’s foot is two bars, so the sway lands on the song.

02 / The machineTheir footage, untouched.

Left: the product
page. Right: the loop.

The listeners and flowers were drawn over a frame of teenage engineering’s product video, animated, then the original footage was put back over the machine on every frame. The disc, counters and floor reflection are theirs.

03 / The labelArt on the spinning disc.

A label made
to be spun.

A pinwheel of trumpet flowers, daisies and leaves all leaning one way, plus one sleeping listener hugging a sneaker, so the rotation reads even at phone size. It turns once per bar, eight times per loop, and returns exactly to where it started.

What it is not

An unsolicited spec concept, not commissioned or approved by teenage engineering. The machine footage is teenage engineering’s own product video from their EP/BC-1 page. The characters, flowers and disc label were generated with AI models (fal.ai: nano-banana 2.1, Kling 4.0) from Isaac’s style references, then directed, retimed, composited and finished by hand. The song is added in-app, not included in the file.

Concept, art direction, compositing and edit: Isaac Hernandez. Illustration and animation generated with AI models under his direction.
