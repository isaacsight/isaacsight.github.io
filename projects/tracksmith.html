Tracksmith

Before the day has a name.

A spec campaign for the Boston running brand built on the amateur spirit. One runner, one ordinary weekday, 5:40 to 7:10 a.m. A 30-second film and a six-frame photo story.

Creative direction · Film direction and edit · Photo story · Casting · Copy
01 / The briefInsight, idea, direction.

The hours
nobody sees.
Why this idea, for this brand.

The brand

Tracksmith honors the amateur spirit, the serious runner without a pro contract. Its look comes from classic American sportswear and early collegiate track, and it deliberately doesn’t build its marketing around elite athletes.

The insight

Amateurs train in the hours nobody watches. Before work, before the kids wake up, before anyone has asked them for anything. The run is the only part of the day that belongs to them.

The idea

Before the day has a name. No race, no medal, no crowd. Just a record of the hour before the day starts, told in timestamps, from a dark bedroom at 5:40 to a train seat at 7:10.

01

Cast an amateur.

A runner around 30 with a real build and an old watch, who ends the film in a wool coat on her commute. The commute makes it believable.

02

Timestamps, not taglines.

The supers are only times and places, set like a race-day program. The clock tells the story, and the line waits for the end.

03

Dark to light.

The grade moves from cold blue streetlight to gold sun on the stadium steps, so the film’s arc and the morning’s arc are the same thing.

02 / The filmSix shots, ninety minutes.

5:40 to 7:10.

Waking in the dark, lacing up in the hall, an empty Marlborough Street at blue hour, the Charles at first light, the Harvard Stadium stands as the sun breaks, then the Red Line with damp hair and a small private smile. Hard cuts, a quiet piano score that lifts with the sun, and a cream end card. The 4:5 cut is the same master for the feed.

03 / Photo storySame runner, same morning.

Captioned
by the clock.

Directed as one shoot on 35 mm color film stock: Portra grain, navy, cream and brick, with the light doing the storytelling. Each frame is captioned only with the time, the way a training log would record it. Apparel is unbranded on purpose: no Tracksmith product, hare or logo is shown or invented.

06 / The takeawayScope & notes.

The film never shows a finish line. That’s the point: for an amateur, the run itself is the win.

Project scope & production notes

What this covers

Rollout plan

Every shot was made from one casting still, so the runner stays the same person across the film and the photo story. One shot was regenerated to remove a logo-like mark from her shorts.

What it is not

An unsolicited spec concept, not commissioned or approved by Tracksmith. The runner is not a real person. All footage, stills and music are AI-generated (fal.ai: nano-banana 2.1, minimax h3, stable-audio 2.5), then art directed, edited and graded by hand. No Tracksmith product, logo or hare appears.
