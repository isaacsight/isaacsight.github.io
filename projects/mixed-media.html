PASSAGE/00

Creative direction for a streetwear label that doesn't exist: one tunnel photo, then chalk, tape and neon drawn on top.

PASSAGE/00 is a made-up streetwear label, and this is its first campaign. It starts with one realistic photo of a figure in a hooded jacket in a tiled underpass. Then it gets drawn on, frame by frame: chalk outlines, arrows, a torn neon cut-out, tape, and a taped-in second photo, with monospace POV captions on black above and below.

The genre

Inspired by mixed-media creative direction reels: a realistic photo layered with drawn and graphic overlays, with monospace POV captions.

The insight

Most creative direction lives in the gap between a plain photo and the finished brand world. This piece shows that gap on screen: the photo stays realistic and untouched, and the brand gets drawn on top of it in real time, as rough notes that turn into the campaign. The label is fictional on purpose. The joke in the captions is that the whole brand exists only in this tunnel and in the marks drawn over it.

01

Keep the photo plain, make the marks rough

The underpass photo gets only a slow push, a little grain and nothing else. All the character comes from the layer on top: displaced chalk strokes that boil every two frames, a torn grainy cut-out, beige tape. Keeping the two layers far apart is what makes it read as mixed media and not a filter.

02

One loud colour

Everything is off-white chalk on green-grey, plus one acid neon (#D4FF3A) for perspective lines and scribbles, and one orange paper cut-out. A single accent tells the eye where to look as the drawing builds.

03

Captions do the strategy

The monospace POV lines carry the brief in plain words: art directing a label that only exists in this tunnel, so you draw the rest in. They type on at the top, the drawing builds through the middle, and the payoff line lands at the bottom.

03 / The processHow it was made.
STEP 01

Base photo

Generated one realistic 9:16 photo with Nano Banana 2.1: a figure in a charcoal hooded jacket in a tiled underpass, with the face lost in shadow. Two more frames of the same figure, walking away and a three-quarter close-up, were made with Nano Banana 2.1 Edit so the set stays consistent.

STEP 02

Overlays as code

Every mark is an SVG path in one HTML file. Chalk texture comes from SVG turbulence and displacement, and the draw-on uses stroke-dashoffset. Torn paper, tape and the taped-in photo are built shapes with a grain filter. Labels use a chalk typeface and captions use Menlo.

STEP 03

Frame-by-frame render

Headless Chrome (via Playwright) rendered 120 transparent overlay frames at 12 fps, a stop-motion rate that suits hand drawing. The noise seed changes every two frames so the strokes boil. The overlay layer scales in step with the photo's push so the outline stays on the figure.

STEP 04

Composite

In ffmpeg: zoompan push (1.00 to 1.08 over 10 s) on a 2× upscale of the photo, light grain, a letterbox onto black, then the overlay frames on top at 30 fps. The first encode with heavier grain came out at 37 MB, so the grain was dialled down to bring the file under 9 MB.

STEP 05

Stills

The same overlay system, with a different set of marks and captions for each of the other two photos, rendered as finished frames.

What it is not

All imagery is AI-generated: photos with Google Nano Banana 2.1 and Nano Banana 2.1 Edit on fal. Overlays are hand-built in SVG/HTML, rendered with headless Chrome and composited in ffmpeg. The figure is generic and does not depict a real person. PASSAGE/00 is a fictional brand. Every frame carries an AI-GENERATED mark. A self-initiated piece inspired by a genre, not a copy of any specific work.
