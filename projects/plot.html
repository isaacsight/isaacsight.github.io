A landscape-architecture studio identity built on a parcel-grid mark, terracotta and a site hoarding that announces the garden before it exists.

01 / The ideaStart with a reason.

A mark that behaves
like a site plan.

The brief

Create an identity for a small landscape-architecture studio that reads as precise and earthy at once, and that can live on a building site for a year before the garden is visible.

The response

A plot is a parcel of ground and a drawing of one. The mark is a small square grid with one cell filled — the site, located. Terracotta, clay and moss are the colors of the material the studio actually works with; the grotesk is set the way annotations are set on a drawing.

01

Make the mark a diagram.

One filled cell in a grid says ‘here, on this ground’ and scales from a debossed card to a forty-foot hoarding without change.

02

Use the hoarding as the launch.

A landscape takes years. The hoarding — ‘A garden is coming’ — turns the waiting into the campaign.

03

Color from the trade.

Terracotta, clay, moss, graphite: pot, soil, planting, pencil. Nothing the studio would have to explain.

Grid, mark, annotation.

The grid mark anchors every surface; the wordmark sits beside or beneath it; all other text is annotation-sized. On site the mark repeats like survey stakes; on paper it appears once.

Fixed

Square-grid mark with one terracotta cell, PLOT wordmark in grotesk, annotation-scale secondary type.

Variable

Which cell is filled (it moves per project), the field color, and the single announcement line.

Format rule

Card: deboss, no color. Drawing: mark in the title block. Hoarding: mark repeated, one line, no small type.
