Breath Weather

A sky made of the day's visitors.

A long wall of cold glass stands in front of a projected sky. Breathe on it and your fog lifts off as a small cloud in the exact shape you left, then drifts away with the day. By closing time the sky is made of everyone who came. An original installation concept, shown as a 90-second film.

Installation concept · Interaction design · Spatial design · Film · Sound
Concept film · 1:30 · sound onGenerated footage of an installation that doesn't exist yet
01 / The ideaLeave something behind.

Every visitor
leaves a cloud.
The room remembers the day.

The question

Most museum interactives react while you stand there and forget you the moment you leave. What if the room kept a trace of everyone, and you could find yours again?

The idea

Your breath becomes weather. Warm breath on cold glass is something everyone has done as a child. Here it does what it always felt like it should: the fog lifts off and becomes a cloud, and it stays in the sky with everyone else's until the museum closes.

The science

Condensation is the same physics as a cloud: warm, wet air meeting something cold. The installation makes that link visible, at the scale of one person and of a whole day.

02 / How it worksSense, shape, release.

A thermal camera
reads the fog.

The glass is kept just cold enough to fog. A thermal camera behind it sees each warm bloom, traces its outline and hands that shape to the sky, where it becomes a cloud. The glass gently warms to clear itself between visitors.

03 / The rulesDesigned, not magic.

A few rules
make it a place.

Every installation is a small set of rules people learn by doing. These are the ones that make Breath Weather readable at a glance and worth coming back to.

01 · One breath, one cloud

Each cloud keeps the exact outline of the fog that made it, so no two are the same.

02 · The sky is a timeline

The sky drifts slowly left to right through the day. New clouds appear on the left; the morning gathers on the right. Where a cloud is tells you when its visitor came.

03 · Find yours again

As your cloud lifts, a ticket slides out of the floor with your time and your cloud's shape. Look up, and find it among hundreds.

04 · The room resets

At closing the lights go down, the sky holds its last light, and the glass clears. Tomorrow starts empty.

04 / Making the filmOne look, then everything matched it.

A look frame,
fourteen stills,
eighteen clips.

The film follows one visitor's cloud through one day, from opening to closing. It started as a written spec: a storyline, eighteen shots and rules for the look (gallery white, soft north light, 35 mm, film grain, and no faces ever shown).

One approved look frame set the room, the glass and the light. Every shot's still was generated from it with fal so the world stays consistent, then animated with Kling. Captions, the ticket, the tag that follows her cloud and the sound were cut together in HyperFrames.

How it was made

A personal concept; the installation and the museum shown don't exist. Stills were generated with fal (nano-banana 2.1) from one approved look frame, animated with Kling 4.0, and cut with captions, graphics and sound in HyperFrames; sound was generated with stable-audio 2.5. Nobody's face appears. Research into existing work (Random International's Rain Room, Rafael Lozano-Hemmer, Fujiko Nakaya's fog sculptures, Marshmallow Laser Feast) shaped where this idea is new: breath on glass becoming a shared, persistent sky.
