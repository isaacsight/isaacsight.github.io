Little Company

Drawings and toys meet real LA streets, one action at a time.

Four ten-second vertical films set in one sunny LA. A shaggy green ink creature and its tiny white pigeon, and Palmbot, a glossy vinyl toy head, show up inside real-looking street places: a crosswalk signal, a tagged roll-up gate, a taco-truck window, a tiled pedestrian tunnel. Each film is one continuous action and ends on a title that snaps on.

01 / The reelFour films joined with hard cuts, 40 s.

Mixed media
as a world.

Mixed media as a world, not a filter. The street is always photoreal; the characters keep their own medium (scratchy ink, glossy vinyl, chalk and neon) and the fun is watching the two meet: the drawing hops out of the signal, the gate rolls up on a toy, the pigeon lands on a real cup.

04 / The processHow every film is made.

Composite in the still,
then animate.

The mixing of media happens before anything moves. The generated still already puts the drawing in the street, and the video model animates the whole frame, so the character and the place move as one shot.

STEP 01

One world bible

The same cast in every film (the green ink creature, its tiny pigeon and Palmbot, the vinyl toy head), the same sunny LA, accents in hot pink, cobalt and signal orange, and one snap-on title: LITTLE COMPANY · Nº 0X in Menlo over the name.

STEP 02

Build the frame

Nano Banana edit places the ink characters on a real-looking walk signal, Palmbot in an ornate doorway and the pigeon on a taco-truck ledge. Keeping the characters flat in the still is what keeps them flat once they move.

Nº 01 Walk: the figure leaves the signal. Nano Banana 2.1 still, Kling 3.0 Pro motion.

STEP 03

Land the reveal

Roll-Up uses a start frame and an end frame: the closed, tagged gate and the same doorway with Palmbot already open. The model bridges the two in one take, so the doorway never changes and the reveal lands.

Nº 02 Roll-Up: start and end frames, one take.

STEP 04

The takes that failed

Two of eight video generations were rejected. In Pour, the model invented a worker whose face appeared in the truck window. In Tunnel, a camera move swept the drawings off the walls. Each got one tighter re-run: only a hand and forearm in the window, and a near-locked camera with drawings that stay put.

Approved Nº 04 Tunnel: a near-locked camera, drawings that stay on the wall.

STEP 05

Sound, type and edit

Native foley only (a gate rattle, footsteps, ice), levels measured on every file. Titles are rendered in headless Chrome and snapped on at the settle point with no fade. The reel joins all four films with hard cuts.

05 / The takeawayScope & notes.

Characters travel when the world around them feels real. Keep the street photoreal and let the drawing stay a drawing.
