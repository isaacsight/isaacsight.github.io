Permanent Records

Dig all day. Stay for the show.

A gig-poster campaign for a record store that becomes a bar and a stage after dark — a vinyl groove turning into a beam of light.

Campaign direction · Hero + story design · Rollout plan
01 / The briefGoal, idea, direction.

Dig all day. Stay for the show.
Why this line, for this business.

The business

Permanent Records Roadhouse in Cypress Park is a record store, bar and live venue in one room — described as the first of its kind in Los Angeles.

The goal

Say the whole proposition in one image: come for the crates, stay for the band. One poster that works for the shop, the bar and any show.

The central idea

The record and the stage are the same object. A vinyl disc, seen edge-on, loses its grooves into a single beam of stage light. Daytime becomes nighttime in one gesture; the headline just says it out loud.

01

One object, two rooms.

No crates, no bar, no crowd. The disc-into-light device carries the store/venue idea on its own so it never misrepresents the space.

02

Gig-poster type.

A heavy condensed grotesk in warm white against black, with small typewriter details — the vernacular of show flyers, tightened up.

03

Tungsten, not neon.

One warm orange beam on a black field. It reads as stage light and as the glow of a bar, and it prints cheaply as two colors.

A poster you can staple anywhere.

The device is reusable: the beam can point at a date, a band name or a sale. Keep the black field and the condensed type, and every show flyer looks like it came from the same room.

Fixed

Black field, disc-to-beam device, condensed grotesk headline, one red rule, typewriter CTA.

Variable

Headline, date line and what the beam illuminates. Beam color can shift warm-to-red for special shows.

Format rule

Landscape puts the disc left and type right. Vertical stacks the beam full-height behind the headline.

What it is not

No commission, client approval or measured results exist for this work. Motion, print and email items in the rollout are proposals only.
