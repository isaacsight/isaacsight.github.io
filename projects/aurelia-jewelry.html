LUMBRE: Desert Collection

A fictional jewelry brand where the product, the model and the glass house in the desert are all generated, with no photo shoot.

LUMBRE (Spanish for firelight) is an invented jewelry line of hand-hammered gold ear cuffs and a wide ring, shaped by low desert sun. The campaign world has a product macro, a cropped model, a hand detail and a glass pavilion in the high desert. A 12-second process film shows how the pieces connect, with callout lines and bilingual Spanish and English labels.

The insight

Small jewelry brands rarely have budget for a location shoot, but AI can build the whole world around one product. The interesting part is consistency: the ring and cuffs have to stay the same object from the macro to the model to the hand. So the film shows the process as well as the result.

The idea

A fictional jewelry brand where the product, the model and the glass house in the desert are all generated, with no photo shoot.

01

One product as the anchor

The product macro came first. The model and hand images were made by editing with that image as the reference, so the hammered surface and shapes carry across every frame.

02

Show the process

Rather than hiding the method, the film lays the images out like a working board. Thin callout lines point at the pieces, and small tags say how each image was made (txt → img, img → img · ref 01).

03

Bilingual labels

Labels pair Spanish and English (producto / product, modelo / model, escena / setting). This fits an LA brand with a Spanish name and gives the board its quiet catalogue rhythm.

02 / The workFrames and cuts.
03 / The processHow it was made.
STEP 01

Product first

Nano Banana 2.1 generated the hero: hammered gold ear cuffs and a wide ring on travertine in low desert light. It was kept from the first pass.

STEP 02

Model and detail from the reference

The model (face turned away, cropped to ear and neck) and the hand on a travertine plinth were made with Nano Banana 2.1 Edit, using the product image as the reference. The setting, a glass pavilion among Joshua trees, was generated separately from text.

STEP 03

Board in HTML

The layout was built in HTML and CSS: panels slide and unmask, SVG callout lines draw in, and labels rise. Each frame was captured at 30 fps by stepping the CSS animations in headless Chromium.

STEP 04

Crossfade to the lockup

ffmpeg crossfades the board into a slow pull-back on the product with the LUMBRE lockup and an AI-GENERATED mark. No video model was needed, so all the motion is designed and none of it morphs.

What it is not

LUMBRE is a fictional brand. All product, model and setting images are AI-generated with Google Nano Banana 2.1 and Nano Banana 2.1 Edit (via fal). Layout and animation were built in HTML/CSS and rendered with headless Chromium and ffmpeg. No real person, product or place is depicted. A self-initiated piece inspired by a genre, not a copy of any specific work.
