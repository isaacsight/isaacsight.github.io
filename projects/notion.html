Notion

Open the right doors.

A spec launch for Notion 3.8, “Your agents and teams run on context.” A workspace is a building, your docs are its rooms, and an agent is a light that only goes where you let it. A 20-second film for business buyers, made to explain a technical feature without overstating it.

Creative direction · Launch film · Storyboard · Copy · Edit
01 / The briefInsight, idea, direction.

Agents that know
the work.
Why this idea, for this brand.

The launch

Notion 3.8 (Oct 13, 2026) lets agents such as ChatGPT and Claude connect to a workspace through MCP, read it and update it. There are 150+ MCP connections. Column permissions hide fields like compensation from people or agents, and admins can see who connected what and revoke it.

The insight

Teams want agents that know the work, and are afraid of agents that know everything. The feature is really two features: access, and limits on access.

The idea

Open the right doors. Draw the workspace as a building. Docs, projects and wikis are rooms. An agent is a beam of light that can only enter the rooms you open, and you can close any door again.

01

A model, not an illustration.

A white foamcore architectural model cut in section, lit from above. It reads as considered and physical, and stays far away from Notion’s own illustration style.

02

Light means access.

Warm light is the only colour in the film. Where the light reaches, the agent can read. Where a door stays shut, it can’t.

03

Say the limit out loud.

Shot five is the one most launch films skip: some rooms stay closed. For a business buyer that is the selling point, not the fine print.

02 / The storyboardSix shots, twenty seconds.

Dark to lit.
Open to closed.

The film opens on the model in the dark, lights the rooms one by one, lets a single beam in through a doorway to update a board, then shows one room that stays shut. Each line on screen says what the product does, in the order a buyer would ask.

03 / The takeawayScope & notes.

The product’s most reassuring feature, a door that stays closed, is the shot the whole film builds to.
