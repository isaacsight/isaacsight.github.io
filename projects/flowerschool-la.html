FlowerSchool

An evening in bloom.

An invitation-style campaign for private group workshops — ink botanicals, midnight aubergine and a line that reads like an RSVP card.

Campaign direction · Hero + story design · Rollout plan
01 / The briefGoal, idea, direction.

An evening in bloom.
Why this line, for this business.

The business

FlowerSchool Los Angeles teaches floral design and offers private workshops for groups, teams and celebrations.

The goal

Make the private-workshop offer feel like an occasion worth booking — the kind of evening a host would want to send an invitation for.

The central idea

Treat the campaign as the invitation itself. Ink botanical drawings frame a generous dark field; an italic serif headline sits where the event name would go. No invented date, no price — just the feeling of an evening that has already been planned beautifully.

01

Draw, don’t photograph.

Fine ink line drawings keep the flowers illustrative and timeless, and leave room for the real arrangements to be the surprise on the night.

02

Invitation hierarchy.

Brand as a small two-line crest, headline in an exquisite italic, CTA as a single underlined line. Everything a printed card would have, nothing it wouldn’t.

03

Dark, warm, quiet.

Midnight aubergine with creamy ivory type and silver-green ink. It reads evening without reading nightclub.

Everything an invitation needs.

The layout mirrors a printed invitation: crest, headline, one line of detail, a botanical border. The same structure carries a public workshop calendar, a private-event inquiry page and the card a host sends to guests.

Fixed

Two-line brand crest, italic serif headline centered, underlined CTA. Ink botanicals confined to the border.

Variable

Density of the border, the headline for the occasion, and an optional date line when a real event exists.

Format rule

Landscape frames the headline with botanicals on both sides. Vertical gathers them top and bottom and brings the CTA closer to the headline.

03 / The completed boardHero + story, as delivered.

Two formats.
One identity.

The landscape hero and the vertical story were designed together so the campaign reads the same on a website banner and on a phone. This is the completed, deliverable artwork; everything in section 05 builds on it.
