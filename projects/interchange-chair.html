Interchange

A lounge chair bent from one sheet of tinted aluminium, its curves taken from an LA freeway interchange.

Interchange is an object concept: a single continuous sheet of anodized aluminium, tinted from oxblood to violet, folded into a lounge chair. The seat rises, banks into the backrest and loops under itself to become the base, the way a ramp climbs, turns and drops back into traffic. It is shot like a sculptural-furniture feed: grey studio sweep, soft daylight, one person and a worn rug for scale.

Why this idea, for this brand.

The genre

Inspired by sculptural bent-sheet-metal furniture studios and how they shoot their work: grey studio sweep, soft daylight, a person and a rug for scale.

The insight

LA's stacked freeway interchanges are the city's most sculptural structures: long ribbons that bank, overlap and loop without a break. The chair borrows that logic, so one band of metal does everything with no joints and no second material. The tint shifts like evening light on the concrete.

The idea

A lounge chair bent from one sheet of tinted aluminium, its curves taken from an LA freeway interchange.

01

One sheet, no joints

Every image keeps the chair as a single unbroken band. Seat, back and base are one gesture, which is the whole point of the interchange reference.

02

Tint instead of chrome

Anodized oxblood to violet keeps the piece warm and a little iridescent against the grey sweep, closer to sculpture than to showroom furniture.

03

Scale without a face

The seated figure is turned away and cropped at the head, on a worn kilim. The rug and body give scale and warmth; the chair stays the subject.

03 / The processHow it was made.
STEP 01

Hero frame

Generated the three-quarter studio view with Nano Banana 2.1 (text to image, 9:16, 2K), prompting a single bent sheet with interchange-ramp curves.

STEP 02

Consistent variations

Used Nano Banana 2.1 Edit with the hero as reference for the side profile, the bent-edge macro and the seated scale shot on a kilim. The side profile reads a little differently from the hero (the rear loop shows as an open foot); it matches what the orbit reveals, but it is worth a second look.

STEP 03

Orbit

Animated the hero with Wan 3.0 Prime image-to-video, prompting a slow camera arc with the chair rigid and unchanged. I checked frames across the clip: the shape holds and the hidden rear foot comes into view as the camera moves. The 5 s clip was slowed 1.6x with motion interpolation to an 8 s orbit.

STEP 04

Type and finish

Built the caption layer in HTML, rendered it with headless Chrome and composited it in ffmpeg with an AI-GENERATED mark.
