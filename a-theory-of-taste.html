A Theory of Taste.

A white panel floats in an empty Los Angeles parking garage. Thirty-two worlds pass through it, pinned to it by tracking and sliced into windows on every bar. On the drop the garage folds like a mirror. Then it all drifts away, and one thing stays on the panel.

How it was madeSix decisions

It started with my own feed, and every rejection became a rule.

I sent around twenty-five reels from my Instagram that I keep coming back to. They were studied frame by frame for their grammar: objects locked in place, tracked overlays, paper collage, one hot colour, cuts on the beat. Then the early versions failed. One was too scary. One read like a cheesy commercial. A page-turn transition read like a book. Each no became a rule the next version had to follow.

01

One panel, one place

A white panel with a coral selection frame, floating in a black-and-white concrete garage. Everything happens on that one surface.

02

32 worlds, really tracked

Each world is a short generated clip with its own movement. Code finds the panel's four corners in every frame and warps the world onto it, so it sits in the space.

03

Slice, don't turn

On every bar the panel splits into windows that flip to the next world, then the film cuts away inside it for a beat before coming back.

04

The garage folds

From the drop on, the structure reflects and bends like a mirror world while the panel holds still at the centre.

05

Cut to the grid

The track is liquid drum and bass at 173.75 BPM. The tempo and the drop were measured, and every slice, flash and cutaway lands on that grid. The breakdown was carved by hand with a filter sweep.

06

An ending that lets go

The windows drift away, the garage unfolds back to calm, the closing line types on, and the panel becomes the title card.

Production notesSelf-initiated, no client

The AI made the pieces. The taste is in what got kept.

What I did, and what AI made

What I did

I chose the reference reels, set the direction and the rules, turned down the versions that didn't work, picked the window slicing, asked for the folding garage, the 1:30 runtime and the two lines, and approved every cut until the final one.

What AI made

Claude (Anthropic) in Claude Code wrote the prompts, the tracking and compositing code in Python and OpenCV, and the Blender edit pass. Images were generated with GPT Image, motion with Kling and the music with ElevenLabs Music, all on fal. No stock footage, no real people filmed. It is a self-initiated concept, AI-assisted under my direction.
